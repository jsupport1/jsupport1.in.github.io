IFSC and MICR Codes: Everything You Need to Know

If you have ever done an online fund transfer or looked at the bottom of your cheque leaf, you would have seen IFSC and MICR codes. These alpha-numeric strings are the backbone of the Indian banking infrastructure.

What is IFSC Code?

The Indian Financial System Code (IFSC) is an 11-character alphanumeric code that uniquely identifies every bank branch participating in the NEFT and RTGS systems. The first four characters represent the bank, the fifth is zero, and the last six characters represent the branch.

What is MICR Code?

The Magnetic Ink Character Recognition (MICR) code is a 9-digit code used to identify a bank and branch participating in an Electronic Clearing System (ECS). It is primarily used for the processing and clearance of cheques.

Why are they important?

  • Error-Free Transfers: They ensure that funds are credited to the correct branch and account.
  • Faster Processing: MICR codes allow for automated cheque processing, reducing manual errors and time.
  • Unique Identification: Even if two branches have similar names, their IFSC and MICR codes will always be different.

Where to find them?

You can find these codes on your cheque book, passbook, or the official website of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
